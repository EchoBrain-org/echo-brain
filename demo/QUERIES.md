# Ask ECHO query set

These are questions about the state of the Echo work, not about meeting
artifacts. Ask them only in Ask ECHO. Do not show a meeting browser, transcript
search, fixture console, or internal approval UI.

## Positioning rule

The demo should make this sentence true without saying it repeatedly:

> ECHO is not where you go to revisit a meeting. ECHO is where you ask what the
> organization has actually decided, why, what happens next, and what you are
> allowed to know.

Avoid prompts such as "summarize the meeting," "find the transcript," "what did
we discuss," or "search my notes." Those prompts put ECHO back in the meeting-tool
category.

## Hero before-and-after query

Audrey — new to the account and present at none of the four meetings — asks
this exact question before approval and again after approval:

> Can we promise Echo that all 28 locations will be live on September 16? What can we safely commit to, what must happen first, and what work remains by when?

Why it matters:

- It does not name or search for a meeting.
- The user does not need to know that the relevant meetings exist.
- It uses everyday words instead of the internal meeting vocabulary.
- No single meeting contains the whole answer.
- The answer must separate prerequisites for the first 10 from evidence required
  for later expansion.
- The same question changes from "insufficient accessible evidence" to a cited,
  actionable answer only after human approval.

This is the primary customer proof. Do not replace it with a narrower question.

## Supporting queries

Use only the queries needed for the time available. Queries 1-4 are asked by Audrey
Ortiz, the team-member persona, after the first three meetings are approved for
Team. Query 5 is the paired privacy proof.

### 1. Customer-safe briefing

> I am speaking with Echo's executive sponsor in five minutes. What can I
> safely promise for September 16, what must I avoid promising, and why?

Shows: a ready-to-use company position, grounded in approved decisions and
rationales rather than a list of meeting summaries. This is Audrey's actual
situation — covering the sponsor call within days of joining the account — so it
carries the emotional core of the demo.

Required answer shape:

- promise only a conditional onboarding window for the first 10;
- do not promise an all-28 launch;
- explain simply that production readiness is still gated and the company does
  not yet have evidence or an operating plan for expansion;
- cite the approved rollout, data-handling, and capacity records as needed;
- do not reveal the private price.

### 2. Readiness and remaining work

> What work remains on Echo and when is it due? Separate what
> is needed for the September 16 onboarding window from what is needed for later
> expansion.

Shows: ECHO turns decisions into coordinated work across Legal, Security, Sales,
and Implementation.

Required answer facts:

- corrected customer wording is due September 3 and confirmation of the first
  10 locations is due September 4;
- capacity assumptions and implementation-team reservation are due September 4;
- the revised addendum is due September 5;
- named-security-contact verification is due September 8;
- the readiness review is due September 12;
- production access waits for the signed addendum and verified security contact.

For later expansion, the adoption dashboard is due September 11, and the first
cohort must then produce four consecutive weeks of qualifying evidence.
The answer must not present that later evidence as something that can be complete
before the September 16 onboarding window.

### 3. Expansion rule

> When can Echo move beyond the first 10 locations, what evidence will we
> use, and what evidence supports that rule?

Shows: durable decision memory with evidence and rationale, expressed without
requiring the user to remember phrases such as "revenue signal calibration."

Required answer facts:

- at least 8 of the first 10 locations;
- four consecutive weekly workflows;
- no manual correction;
- the adoption dashboard is due September 11;
- expansion is reviewed only after the four-week evidence exists.

### 4. Cross-functional action view

> Give me the current Echo commitments and deadlines. Include only
> approved information I am allowed to see.

Shows: one permission-aware operating view across meetings and departments.

For a normal team member, the answer may include Team-approved actions and
dates. It must not include the private order-form action or hint that a private
commercial decision exists.

### 5. Same question, different authorization

Ask as Audrey, the normal team member:

> What special price did Echo receive?

Required answer: `Insufficient accessible evidence to answer this question.`,
with no citation and no hint that a private price record exists.

Ask the exact same question from the authenticated account of the person who
approved Commercial exception review as Only me.

Required answer facts:

- $22 per location;
- first 10 locations only;
- 30-day evaluation only;
- standard pricing afterward unless Finance approves a new exception;
- cite Commercial exception review.

Shows: approved knowledge is not automatically shared knowledge. Authorization is
enforced when the answer is assembled, not merely when a meeting is opened.

## What the customer should notice

Across the sequence, the customer never has to:

- remember a meeting title;
- know a relevant conversation happened;
- guess the words used in that conversation;
- open several summaries and reconcile them;
- decide whether a suggestion became a decision;
- manually check whether the answer is safe for them to see.

The meetings are evidence. The product surface is approved organizational truth.
